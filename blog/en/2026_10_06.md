The website has moved and been rebuilt. It's now called **iobroker.com** , and anyone who's already visited it will have noticed the difference in appearance. But there's a lot hidden beneath the surface that you won't find unless someone points it out. That's precisely what this is about.

## Light and dark

The page appears dark, just like the admin interface. If you prefer a lighter reading experience, open the menu in the top right corner behind the three lines: there you'll find the sun and moon icon. One click flips the entire page, including the documentation, blog, and adapter list. Your choice is saved in the browser and will be displayed again on your next visit.

## The search knows everything

Press **Ctrl+K** anywhere on the page **(⌘K** on a Mac). A window will open that simultaneously searches the documentation, adapters, and blog.

<img src="de/blog/images/2026_10_06_suche.webp" width="800" style="border: 1px solid #205895"/>

The results are grouped by category, and the total number of results is displayed below. The entire interface is controlled via the keyboard: use the arrow keys to navigate, press Enter to open, and Escape to close. The interface remembers your recent searches, so you don't have to type again next time.

For those who prefer clicking: the magnifying glass icon in the top left does the same thing.

## Adapter overview

There are currently **over 800 adapters** listed. You can filter by category on the left. Each category shows how many adapters are in that category: 90 under Energy, 69 under Hardware, 66 under Climate Control.

<img src="de/blog/images/2026_10_06_adapter.webp" width="800" style="border: 1px solid #205895"/>

Each tile displays the stable version, the rating, and the number of installations. The three small icons below lead to GitHub, the README, and statistics that reveal more than you might expect:

<img src="de/blog/images/2026_10_06_versionen.webp" width="800" style="border: 1px solid #205895"/>

This is the distribution of installed versions. In BackItUp, the largest block is on the current version 4.0.3, but the second largest is still on the older 3.3.17. This is a useful figure for adapter authors and a good opportunity for everyone else to check if they are among the laggards.

You can switch between tile and list view in the top right corner.

## The documentation

The documentary is available in **three languages** : German, English, and Russian. German is the source material; the rest is derived from it.

<img src="de/blog/images/2026_10_06_doku.webp" width="800" style="border: 1px solid #205895"/>

Four things that make everyday life easier:

- The field above the text **filters the chapters** in the sidebar. If you know the topic is backup, you can type that in and only see relevant content. Pressing Enter searches the text itself.
- **In the upper right corner of this page,** a table of contents for the currently open page will appear.
- Each heading has its **own anchor** . When you reply in the forum, you can use this to link directly to the relevant paragraph instead of the entire page.
- **Images can be clicked** and then displayed in a larger size. Within the text, they are displayed at column width. Previously, the wide diagrams and screenshots from the admin panel were too small to read.

## The statistics

The part where most people get stuck longer than they intended.

<img src="de/blog/images/2026_10_06_statistik.webp" width="800" style="border: 1px solid #205895"/>

At the top are four charts: the number of installations, the number of adapters in use, the number of installations running across multiple hosts, and the date of the figures. Below are countries, operating systems, node versions, and a map.

Two things to note, since they are asked about this regularly. The figures are **reported voluntarily** ; every installation can disable this, and none of it is personally identifiable. Also, the map comes from Google Maps, which is why it only loads when you request it: loading it sends your IP address to Google, and this shouldn't happen without your consent.

## The blog

You're reading this right now. It has **an RSS feed for each language** ; for German, [it's blog\_de.xml](https://www.iobroker.com/blog_de.xml) . Anyone using a feed reader won't miss anything.

The posts are categorized, such as Review, Announcement, or News. Each post gets its own preview image when shared. So, if you post a link in the chat or forum, the actual title image of the post will appear, not some placeholder.

And so you don't miss anything without having to constantly check: next to **"Blog"** in the header of the page, a small blue dot appears as soon as a new post is published that you haven't seen yet. Once you've visited the blog overview, it disappears again. Nothing is saved except a value in your browser; no account, no login.

## The brand for download

A new page showcases our [brand and visual identity](/docs/about/brand.md) . The logo, wordmark, and combined version are available for download there as SVG and PNG files, all in a single ZIP archive, including the two shades of blue with their values and the fonts.

This is intended for everyone who creates something using the name: adapter authors, presentations, videos, press. Now nobody has to cut a logo out of a screenshot anymore.

## The license page

At the top of every page is **the heading "Licenses,"** and behind it lies more than just a price list. Five sections at the top serve as an introduction: Remote Access, Voice Control, Services, Visualization, and Licenses. Below these are separate sections for each of these: Remote Access, Smart Assistant and Services, vis-2, JägerDesign Widgets, and KNX.

Each section answers the questions that usually end up in the forum. Which license do you actually need for vis-2, and when do you need the commercial one? What are the benefits of an offline license? Do you need one for KNX? What happens when remote access expires? The answer is reassuring: ioBroker continues to run locally, including all automations; only external access ends. And the most frequently asked question has its own paragraph: iobroker.net or iobroker.pro?

The prices, durations, and data point levels on the cards come directly from the shop. Anything that's currently unavailable isn't shown. During a promotion, the card displays the promotional price instead of the regular price.

How the two types of licenses work, what they depend on and why the normal case doesn't need any at all, is explained in the documentation under ["Licenses Overview](/docs/licenses/README.md) ".

## One profile for all licenses

Your profile is located behind the person icon in the top right corner, and that's where the biggest change is.

Anyone who has ever purchased a vis license and later set up the voice assistant knows the question: Was that on **iobroker.net** or **iobroker.pro** ? Two servers, two logins, two interfaces that had little to do with each other. Anyone wanting to check what they actually owned had to log in twice and figure out where to look first.

That's over now. There's **a profile** now, and everything is in there. The menu on the left is the same on both servers and lists the two license types directly below each other:

<img src="de/blog/images/2026_10_06_profil.webp" width="800" style="border: 1px solid #205895"/>

- Selecting **"Applications"** leads to your own installation. The icon next to it tells you whether a connection to it currently exists.
- **The app key** is the key that the app and external services use to log in.
- **Adapter licenses** are those for vis-2, KNX, and the JägerDesign widgets. They are linked to the serial number of your installation.
- **Access licenses** are those for remote access and voice assistants. They are linked to the account.
- **Settings** refers to the account settings, including the option to delete the profile.
- **Guest access** grants another person access to individual instances, optionally for a limited time. This is useful if someone needs help with setup and you don't want to give them your password.
- **Diagnostics** show consumption, runtime, and other information related to your access.

Previously, half of the list was on one server and the other half on another. Now you see the same list regardless of where you log in.

If you access one of the old addresses, i.e., **iobroker.net** or **iobroker.pro** , you will also land directly in the new profile. Old bookmarks will continue to work; they just look different than before.

You don't need to do anything. Your licenses, subscriptions, and settings are where they were before; they're just displayed in one place. If you have an account on both servers and use different passwords for each, that will remain the case.
